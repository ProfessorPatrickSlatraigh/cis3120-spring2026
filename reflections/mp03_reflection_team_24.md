## Comparative Reflection

The integrated map reveals a striking asymmetry between how Financial Services
and Travel and Hospitality companies disclose location-related activity in their
SEC filings. Travel and Hospitality companies produced five geocoded events
across a 360-day window, clustered in major cities including Las Vegas, New York,
Houston, and Huntsville. Financial Services produced only one geocoded event
over the same period.

This disparity reflects a fundamental difference in each industry's relationship
to physical location. Hotel and cruise companies file 8-Ks that explicitly
reference new properties and brand expansions because these events are material
to investors tracking expansion strategy. A new Hyatt property in Brooklyn or
a new Royal Caribbean beach club represents incremental revenue that shareholders
monitor closely. The geographic pattern skewing toward leisure and business
travel hubs in the Northeast and South is consistent with post-pandemic demand
recovery concentrated in urban and tourism-friendly markets.

Financial Services companies, by contrast, rarely disclose individual branch
openings or closures as standalone 8-K events. Branch network changes at large
banks like JPMorgan or Bank of America are typically disclosed in quarterly
earnings supplements or investor presentations rather than as standalone 8-K
exhibits. When location events do appear, they tend to involve premium service
locations such as the American Express Centurion Lounge in Las Vegas, which
targets high-spending cardholders in high-traffic leisure destinations. This
structural difference in disclosure practice, not a difference in actual
location activity, explains the low event count for Financial Services.

The geographic contrast the assignment anticipated, Finance consolidating in
lower-cost geographies versus Hospitality expanding into leisure markets, could
not be fully demonstrated given our event counts. However, the single Financial
Services event in Las Vegas and the cluster of Travel and Hospitality openings
in Northeast and Sun Belt cities is directionally consistent with that hypothesis.

The primary limitation of this analysis is the mismatch between our search
strategy and the actual disclosure practices of large-cap financial institutions.
Future iterations could expand the ticker universe to include regional banks,
which file more granular branch-level 8-Ks, or incorporate alternative data
sources such as FFIEC branch filings for a more complete picture of Financial
Services location activity.

## 7. Comparative Reflection

The final map suggests that Financial Services and Travel and Hospitality deploy physical capacity in fundamentally different ways. Financial Services events are largely branch, office, and financial-center announcements from regional and community bank locations tied to local customer access, deposit gathering, commercial relationships, and market coverage. Even as many financial services have went online, banks continue to rely on physical presence to build trust and serve specific local customers.

Travel and Hospitality events appear more closely tied to destination demand and customer experience. The final layer includes casino, hotel, entertainment venue, and hospitality-property activity, projects that add capacity where companies expect people to gather, stay, visit, or spend leisure dollars. This gives the geography a character shaped more by tourism, entertainment districts, regional growth markets, and large physical venues.


The window experiments also reveal a detection difference worth noting. Financial Services reached the 8-event target within a 30-day window, while Travel and Hospitality required a full 180 days. This does not necessarily mean finance generates more location activity overall. It more likely reflects that the Financial Services phrase and ticker list surfaced relevant branch filings more quickly, while Travel and Hospitality announcements were simply less frequent among the selected public companies.


Several limitations apply. The pipeline geocodes US locations only, so international hotels, routes, resorts, offices, and branches may be missing from the map. The strict ticker filter improved classification accuracy, but it also means results depend heavily on the team's chosen ticker lists, and valid industry events from companies outside those lists are intentionally excluded. Even so, the comparison supports the central interpretation Financial Services geography reflects local market coverage and branch economics, while Travel and Hospitality geography reflects the placement of customer-facing capacity where travel and entertainment demand already exists.
